First recognition of a stolen baby as a victim of Francoism

El Correo reported on 15 July 2026 that the Basque Country had recognised a “stolen baby” as a victim of Francoism for the first time. The article, written by Alba Cárcamo, explained the recognitions granted to Olmo Gómez Aldaz by Gogora and the Government of Spain.

A story published in two stages

Alba Cárcamo published an earlier version of the story in El Norte de Castilla on 14 July 2026. That article explained that the Basque Government and the central Government had recognised Olmo Gómez Aldaz, a resident of Tudela de Duero, as a victim of Francoism because he had been taken and adopted without the free and legitimate consent of his parents.

In that first publication, the pioneering nature of the decisions was still presented as Olmo Gómez Aldaz’s own assessment: he “believes it is the first” recognition of its kind granted by both the Basque and central governments.

The following day, El Correo made that fact the central focus of the story. The print edition ran the headline: “The Basque Country recognises a ‘stolen baby’ as a victim of Francoism for the first time” and stated that this was the first decision of its kind, according to confirmation provided to the newspaper by both administrations.

Recognition by Gogora

Gogora’s decision of 6 July 2026 recognised Olmo Gómez Aldaz as a victim of the military coup and the Franco dictatorship under Basque Law 9/2023 on Historical and Democratic Memory.

The decision applied the specific category established in Article 4.2(l) of the Basque law: minors taken from their parents and placed for adoption without their parents’ consent.

The report in El Correo added a further fact: this was the first time the Basque Country had granted a recognition of this kind. In July 2026, Olmo Gómez Aldaz was therefore the first and only person recognised by Gogora under this specific category.

Recognition by the Government of Spain

On 19 February 2026, the Government of Spain had issued a Declaration of Personal Recognition and Reparation under Law 20/2022 on Democratic Memory. The document established that Olmo Gómez Aldaz had been taken and adopted in 1971 without the free and legitimate consent of his parents and that he should be considered a victim of the Franco dictatorship.

On 28 July 2026, the Directorate-General for Victim Support also confirmed, in a decision issued in response to a freedom-of-information request, that this was the only state declaration granted to a person who had been taken, separated or handed over at birth without the free and legitimate consent of their parents.

Two distinct and compatible recognitions

The two administrative acts were issued by different institutions under different laws. The Government of Spain recognises the persecution, removal and adoption without consent under Law 20/2022. Gogora recognises victim status under the specific category established in Article 4.2(l) of Basque Law 9/2023.

Neither act annuls the adoption by itself or determines criminal or financial liability. Their importance lies in the fact that two public authorities formally recognise what happened and place the removal and adoption without consent within democratic-memory policy.

As of July 2026, both recognitions were the first and only ones of their respective kind: the state recognition, according to the Ministry’s subsequent written confirmation, and the Basque recognition, according to the confirmation reported by El Correo.
